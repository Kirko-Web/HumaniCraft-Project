Ecological Solidarity through Upcycling

Behind every recycled material is a person.

Waste pickers play an important role in keeping recyclable materials from ending up in dumps, streets, and waterways. Yet their work is often overlooked. In Barangay 105, Tondo, Manila, ecological solidarity means recognizing their contributions, understanding their struggles, and supporting a cleaner environment for everyone.

01 · Masid-Danas

The people behind waste recovery

Waste pickers and informal recyclers collect and sort materials that can still be reused or sold. Their work contributes to recycling and helps reduce the amount of waste left in the environment. In communities such as Barangay 105, Tondo, these workers are part of the local waste economy, but their working conditions and contributions deserve more attention.

♻️

Helping the environment

Waste pickers recover materials such as bottles, plastics, cardboard, and metals. By sorting and collecting reusable materials, they help keep valuable resources in circulation instead of allowing them to become waste.

🧤

Challenging working conditions

Informal waste workers may work in difficult surroundings and face exposure to dirty materials, unsafe spaces, and limited access to proper equipment or protection.

🤝

Often overlooked

Their contribution to recycling is important, but informal workers may lack recognition, stable income, and support. Environmental programs should consider their needs and voices.

Waste pickers are not the cause of society's waste problem. They contribute to recovering materials and deserve dignity, respect, and fair treatment.
02 · Suri-Nilay · Social Analysis

Why are waste workers vulnerable?

Their struggles are connected to poverty, limited opportunities, informal employment, environmental conditions, and gaps in waste management systems.

1

Unstable livelihood

Income may depend on the quantity and value of materials collected, making it difficult to maintain financial stability.

2

Health and safety concerns

Working around discarded materials and polluted areas can expose workers to risks, especially when protective equipment and facilities are limited.

3

Lack of recognition

Informal recycling workers help communities recover waste, yet their work is not always recognized or included in formal environmental programs.

A cleaner environment should not come at the expense of the dignity and welfare of the people working to achieve it.

Waste management should not depend only on the efforts of informal workers. Government agencies, businesses, communities, and consumers also have a responsibility to reduce waste, improve collection systems, and provide better support for those involved in recycling.

Recognizing waste pickers means treating them as people contributing to environmental protection, not simply as individuals earning from discarded materials.

03 · Suri-Nilay · Theological Reflection

Respecting people is part of caring for creation.

Catholic Social Teaching and Lasallian values remind us that environmental responsibility is connected to human dignity, solidarity, and justice.

🌍

Care for creation

Protecting the environment means being responsible for the resources entrusted to us. Reducing waste and reusing materials are ways of showing care for our shared home.

🫂

Human dignity

Every worker deserves respect, regardless of their occupation or economic situation. Waste pickers deserve safe conditions and recognition for their efforts.

🤝

Solidarity

Solidarity means standing with others and recognizing their struggles. Supporting waste workers is part of building a community where no one is ignored.

Reflection question

How can we practice ecological solidarity not only by reusing materials, but also by respecting and supporting the people who collect and recover them?

04 · Taya-Kilos

Turning waste into opportunity

Upcycling can be a way to promote environmental awareness and creativity while recognizing the knowledge of waste workers. Community activities should respect their participation, experience, and livelihood.

🪴

Bottles into planters

Clean plastic bottles can be reused as plant containers for small gardens or greening projects.

🧺

Fabric into reusable bags

Old shirts and clean fabric scraps can be turned into reusable bags that help reduce single use plastics.

📦

Cardboard into organizers

Clean cardboard can be reused to create storage organizers, dividers, and other useful items.

Upcycling is only one part of proper waste management. Waste workers should not be expected to handle unsafe or contaminated materials without appropriate protection.
Community action

How can we show solidarity?

Supporting waste workers goes beyond making recycled crafts. It also means listening, recognizing their contribution, and encouraging better conditions for their work.

1

Recognize their contribution

Raise awareness about how waste pickers help recover recyclable materials and support waste reduction.

2

Listen to their experiences

Give waste workers a chance to share their concerns, knowledge, and ideas when planning environmental activities.

3

Promote responsible upcycling

Reuse clean materials and avoid creating more waste through poorly planned projects.

4

Support fair treatment

Encourage safer working conditions, fair opportunities, and community programs that include informal recyclers.
